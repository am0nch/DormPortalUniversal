Introduction
▾ About This Handbook

A student residence hall is more than just a place to sleep; it is a happy place where students live and learn. A residence hall setting provides a wonderful opportunity for personality and character development. Living in the residence halls provides a supportive academic and social learning environment and a place to learn vital inter-personal communication skills for community living.

Living in a residence hall means living in a community. Community living requires respect, flexibility, and responsibility from all members. Staff and students must work together to ensure that the community living is positive and educational for everyone.

The following guidelines are listed in alphabetical order and are to assist you in making a quick adjustment to residence hall life. Some guidelines exist to preserve and enhance the unique mission, message and lifestyle of the Seventh-day Adventist church.

ℹ️You may not always like or agree with all the rules and regulations; nevertheless, you are required to abide by them. The Residence Hall Handbook will be updated as needed from time to time.

Rooms
▾ Room Basics

Rooms in all residence halls, except Solomon Hall, are double occupancy with a shared suite bathroom. A resident rents only half of the room; unoccupied spaces may be filled at any time. Single occupancy is possible but rent is double the standard rate.

  • The Dean assigns one or two students to each room.
  • Students are not allowed to change rooms without the Dean's permission.
  • Students must provide their own towels, bedding, pillows, and toiletries.
  • Students are responsible for their room's orderliness and cleanliness.
  • Students are responsible for the conduct of visitors in their room.
  • Students are not to enter other students' rooms when residents are absent.
  • A student planning to move to non-dormitory housing must give at least one month's notice in writing to the Dean.
⚠️Unauthorized room change: 1,000 Baht fine. Students must move back to the originally assigned room.

Residency Requirement
▾ Who Must Live On Campus

The University requires students to live in residence hall units until all available spaces are assigned, prioritizing freshmen and proceeding through sophomore, junior, senior. Emphasis is on students who have not completed six semesters of coursework.

Waivers are available for: students with 6+ semesters completed; students commuting from parent/guardian within 50 km; students with physical/emotional disabilities; married students; mature self-supporting students; academic internship participants.

ℹ️All waiver requests must be submitted in writing to the Residential Hall Director or VPSA.

Room Nightly Check
▾ Curfew & Nightly Check Procedure

The residence halls close at 10:00 p.m. Sun–Fri and 11:00 p.m. Saturday. All outside doors are locked during closed hours (until 5:00 a.m.). A room-by-room night check is conducted 15 minutes after closing time each evening.

  • If not in your room at check time, contact the RA on your floor — it is your responsibility to get checked in.
  • Once inside, get the Dean's permission before leaving again (must be obtained before the Dean leaves for the night).
  • Students working late must submit a petition form to the Dean for an exemption.
⚠️Failure to comply with curfew and nightly check-in policies results in disciplinary action.

Room Changes
▾ Room Change Policy

The University promotes room stability and discourages changes once occupancy is assumed. Each resident is allowed ONE room change per year. To request a room change:

  1. Obtain permission from the Dean of each hall affected.
  2. File a completed room change request form with the Dean.
  3. Pay a 500 Baht room change fee.

Room changes are only considered after all conflict resolution attempts (via RA mediation) are exhausted.

⚠️Unauthorized room change: 1,000 Baht fine + student required to move back to original room.

▾ University Right of Entry

While privacy is respected, the University reserves the right to enter and inspect a student's room whenever deemed necessary. This includes inspection of locked containers and checks for illegal substances, firearms, alcohol, tobacco, fire hazards, and cleanliness.

By accepting a room, the student acknowledges and authorizes this procedure. The Dean may also remove decorations not in harmony with the ethical and moral standards of the University.

Roommate Information
▾ Roommate Relationships

Roommate relationships evolve most successfully when residents demonstrate mutual respect and communicate directly and honestly. In the event of a vacancy, a new roommate may be assigned at any time — all residents are responsible for maintaining the vacancy and welcoming newly assigned roommates.

💡If you have a conflict with your roommate, speak to your RA first — they are trained to help through mediation. Only after all attempts at resolution will room change requests be considered.

Helpful hints: Have realistic expectations. Speak up calmly when something bothers you. Don't let annoyances accumulate. Listen openly. Be willing to compromise. Demonstrate courtesy consistently.

Fire Safety & Equipment
▾ Fire Safety Rules
  • Tampering with fire safety equipment or falsely setting off a fire alarm is a violation of the law and will result in disciplinary action.
  • Open flames and open heating elements are prohibited: candles, incense, hot plates, etc.
  • Flammable materials not allowed: petroleum fuel, motorized vehicles, gasoline-fueled machinery, explosives, fireworks, firecrackers.
▾ Fire & Evacuation Procedure

When alarm sounds:

  1. Evacuate the building immediately — use stairwells only. Do NOT use lifts.
  2. Alert others on your way out.
  3. Close (but don't lock) doors and windows as the last person leaves a room.
  4. Proceed to the designated safety zone (Football Field for student residences).
  5. Do not re-enter until told it is safe by the Emergency Response Team.

If fire is small: Use the nearest fire extinguisher and call for help. If fire is large: Activate the nearest alarm pull station, warn others, call Security/Dean, then evacuate.

⚠️Do not jump from windows unless on the first floor. Do not delay to carry heavy belongings — an extra minute could cost your life.
▾ Fire Drill Procedures
  1. Follow the nearest exit in an orderly, quiet manner. No shouting. Use stairways only.
  2. Proceed to the designated safety zone. Stop no less than 100 meters (50 ft) from any building.
  3. Teachers, Deans, Department Heads: close windows, turn off lights, pull doors before going to meeting point.
  4. If an exit route is blocked, choose another way — always know more than one exit.
  5. Do not return until permitted by the Emergency Response Team designee.
  6. At the meeting point, teachers/deans take an accurate headcount and report to the ERT member in charge.

Alcohol & Drinking
▾ Zero-Tolerance Policy

On or off-campus consumption, possession, or distribution of alcohol is not permitted by students enrolled at AIU. Full, partially full, or empty alcoholic beverage containers are not permitted anywhere in or around the residence halls.

  • If a resident does not claim responsibility for containers found in their room, all roommates will be held accountable.
  • Students are held accountable for allowing others to consume or store alcohol in their room.

  • Staff reserve the right to stop people from bringing alcohol into the building and to search backpacks/bags at entry.
⚠️See the Student Guidebook regarding addictive and controlled substances for additional information and consequences.

▾ Harassment Policy

Harassment is any uninvited, unwelcome, unreciprocated behavior that may cause upset, humiliation, or fear and interferes with a person's right to live, work, and study in a non-threatening environment.

AIU considers harassment unacceptable and aims to provide a learning environment free of harassment. Complaints will be dealt with promptly, confidentially, and according to principles of natural justice. Victimization as a result of a complaint will not be tolerated.

Sexual Harassment
▾ Definition & Reporting

Sexual harassment includes gender-related verbal or physical conduct when: (1) it affects academic evaluation or recommendation; (2) it creates an intimidating, hostile work or learning environment; or (3) it interferes with the victim's social relationships.

Examples: pressure for sexual activity, sexually explicit statements or jokes, repeated leering, unwanted touching, obscene notes, or display of sexually explicit materials.

⚠️Any student found guilty of sexual assault or harassment will be dealt with by the University and face possible prosecution in criminal court. Report incidents to the Dean, VPSA, faculty, staff, or the Counseling Center.

▾ Evening Leave

Students wishing to leave campus after 8:00 p.m. must receive prior permission from the Dean by filling out a Campus Leave Request Online Form/Slip, or obtain permission from the VPSA. Use the Online Notification System for off-campus day trips (7:00 a.m.–8:00 p.m.) as found in the Student Guidebook.

▾ Overnight Leave

Any time you leave campus overnight (including school-sponsored trips), follow the Online Notification System for Overnight Trips. Leaves must be approved by the Dean before leaving.

  • Parental approval is required for freshmen and may be required for any student.
  • Falsification of an overnight leave may result in disciplinary action including possible suspension.
▾ Weekend Leave

Only one weekend leave off-campus per month for all students. Freshmen are not eligible. Students with CGPA ≥ 2.25, attendance ≥ 70%, and citizenship points ≥ 70% (at Dean's discretion) may receive permission to leave twice per month.

  • Weekend leave requests must be submitted at least 24 hours in advance (no later than Thursday noon).
  • Visiting friends/relatives (not parents) counts against the total allowed leave for that month.
▾ Extended Vacation & Summer Leave

For absences of several days, weeks, or months, fill out a permission slip signed by your Dean. Failure to notify the Deans or Student Administration may result in loss of citizenship points or disciplinary measures.

Leaving for summer vacation — checklist:

  1. Contact your Dorm Dean.
  2. Move all belongings to the storage room (Esther Hall ground floor).
  3. Clean the room, bathroom, and balcony.
  4. Return keys to the Dean's office.
⚠️Keeping the room over summer vacation: 4,000 Baht. Students must still register for classes or work at least 20 hours/week. Rooms left uncleaned: cleaning cost charged to the student's account.
ℹ️Freshmen, sophomores, and juniors must vacate rooms 3 days after final exams by 4:00 p.m. Graduating seniors: by 4:00 p.m. on the first Wednesday after Commencement. Room deposit for returning students (1st semester): 2,500 Baht (refunded upon return).

Accidents & Sickness
▾ Medical Care Procedures

If you feel ill or are involved in a minor accident, first attempt to contact your Dean. Each dorm has a first-aid box. The Campus Nurse's office is in the Student Center — call ahead to confirm availability.

☀️ During Office Hours (8:30 a.m.–5:00 p.m.)

  • Minor condition: Dean/RA uses First-Aid Kit or sends to Nurse's Office.
  • Major condition: Nurse visits the dormitory.
  • Emergency: Dean contacts Nurse and/or takes student to clinic or hospital.

🌙 After Office Hours / During Night Check

  • Minor: Dean/RA uses Dorm First-Aid Kit.
  • Major: Dean consults Campus Nurse on whether to send to hospital.
  • Emergency: Dean takes student directly to clinic or hospital.
⚠️Students must contact the campus nurse on duty to be eligible for medical expense reimbursement. Transportation expenses are charged to the student. Medical expenses from alcohol/drug use or traffic violations are not covered.

Abandoned Property
▾ Abandoned Property Policy

Abandoned property refers to student belongings stored in the dormitory by a student who intended to continue at AIU but did not return.

  • Abandoned property will be collected, labeled, and stored by Residence Staff.
  • Held in storage for no less than 12 months, after which it will be disposed of under the direction of the Dean.
  • AIU and its staff are not responsible for damage or theft of abandoned property while in storage.
  • The Dean's office will make minimal attempts to contact the believed owner.

Infectious Disease
▾ Infectious Disease Policy

Since the Residence Hall is a communal place, the University maintains strict policies on infectious diseases (e.g., meningitis, chickenpox, measles, SARS, HIV/AIDS, TB, STDs).

  • Inform the Resident Dean immediately.
  • The School Nurse or Dean may require immediate medical attention.
  • Necessary precautions to prevent further spread must be practiced; quarantine may be required.
  • AIU reserves the right to compel a student to take a leave of absence or withdraw during the contagious period.
  • Approval to return may only be granted by the Campus Nurse under guidance from Student Administration.

Deans & RAs
▾ Staff Roles & Responsibilities

The Dean of Men and Dean of Women, Resident Assistants (RAs), Monitors, and Janitors are responsible for the student residences. They assist in every way possible to make residence life orderly and pleasing for all residents.

Resident Assistant
▾ Role of the RA

One of the most helpful and resourceful persons you will interact with is your Resident Assistant (RA). Located on every floor, RAs are usually sophomore standing or above selected for this leadership position. The RA is your primary resource for information, assistance, and support.

ℹ️RAs assist the Dean in hall administration. Their instructions and requests must be obeyed. If you have a complaint about an RA's instructions, comply first and then take the matter up with the Dean.

Residence Hall Council
▾ RHC Structure & Role

The Residence Hall Council (RHC) is an elected body representing residents' wishes, promoting religious environment, and organizing social activities. Comprises: President, VP, Religious Affairs Coordinator, Treasurer, Secretary, and Social Affairs Coordinator.

RHC representatives report information from the Dean's/VPSA's office, raise funds, mentor students, conduct peer counseling, serve as student voice for policy suggestions, and program large-scale events such as the open house. (Optional for each dormitory.)

Valuables
▾ Protecting Valuables

The University assumes no responsibility for loss or damage to valuables. Best practices:

  • Lock your safe drawer at all times.
  • Lock your room whenever you are absent.
  • Take valuable personal belongings home over breaks.
  • Never leave travel tickets, cash, or electronics at the front desk — deliver in person.
  • Consider purchasing personal property insurance coverage for theft.
  • Keep a list of your electronics' serial numbers.
